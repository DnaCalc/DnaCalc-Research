YIELD stopping: design note (round 44, GPT-6.1 Sol (Codex), dna-firehorse)

Sources: original Excel 16.0.20527 question-service answers; elementary
zero-coupon bond mathematics and exact powers of two. Implementer;
no tier 3/4 implementation source or excluded checkout was read.

R35 and R41 already refuted RATE-style secants, bracket variants and fixed
iteration labels. A new separating design scales both price and redemption
by the same exact power of two, with zero coupons and settlement on a
coupon date. Then every mathematical root is unchanged, and a conventional
price calculation scales exactly. A relative-residual or yield-step stop
with scale-independent seeds would preserve the answer bits.

45 designs: frequencies 1/2/4, maturities 2/4/8 years, discount bases
1.001/1.05/1.25/1.5/2; 17 joint scales 2^-400 through 2^400, 765 questions
(758 numeric, 7 errors; every error remains in the denominator).
Every design changes answer under scaling. Some tiny-price cases return
#NUM or a spurious negative root; those remain failures, not numeric passes.
For n=2, frequency=1, price=25, redemption=100, the true root is exactly 1.
At scale 2^-400 Excel returns exactly 1; at scale 1 it is 2.69e-11 below;
at scales 2^100,2^200,2^400 it is 5.97e-11 below. Large cash scale plainly
does not force an absolute price residual below 1e-10. Thus neither a
plain absolute-price stop nor the scale-invariant complete solver above
explains the map. Initialisation, bracketing, or a mixed stopping test
depends on cash magnitude; a yield-width stop can still explain the
one-sided roughly 1e-10 errors.
No tolerance or seed is identified and no solver change is justified.
Next useful design: map the scale transitions with a fixed exact dyadic
root, then separate seed/bracket changes before fitting a stopping constant.
