Sources: Excel 16.0 build 20527 answers through the question service;

The bank holds no ODDFYIELD or ODDFPRICE rows; the corpus's 30 larger cells
are one bond, ODDFYIELD(39763,44256,39736,39873,0.0575,84.5,100,2,0): Excel
0x3fb3c65d22ec15b6, calc-func 0x3fb3c65d22eca7af (bisection to adjacent

84.50000000036.

Fresh map, 336 random odd-first bonds (frequency 1/2/4, bases 0-4):
- Excel stops short of the root on 328: |price residual| from 1e-13 to 9e-8.
- The residual divided by the price slope (Excel's own ODDFPRICE, central
  difference) is below 1e-10 on every one (largest 9.85e-11), spread
  log-uniformly down to 1e-15: the answer is an iterate whose next Newton
  step would be under 1e-10. 321 of 336 lie below the root (price convex in
  yield, Newton from the left).
- So calc-func's full convergence misses almost every call, though the
  corpus shows only one.
Start: Excel does not return the start exactly when the price is priced at
the rate, 0, 0.01, 0.05, 0.1, 0.5 or 1 (40 bonds each: 0-2 exact). Running
Newton backwards from Excel's answers with central-difference slopes gives
earliest iterates (no predecessor at a yield >= 0) that match no closed form
tried (rate, YIELD on the same dates, the classic average-price
approximations, one step from zero). From the corpus bond's reconstructed
start 0.0401383104, forward Newton with slope step 1e-7 lands within 8 ulp
of Excel's answer; from 0.0575 it converges to the root.
Zero-coupon design (rate 0, same dates, price exactly 100(1+y/2)^-T,
T = 24.6111, 100 prices from 60 to 96.6): up to price 61.11 Excel's answer
is the root to 1e-16; from 61.48 it is short by 8.9e-11, shrinking smoothly
to the root's noise by 83; a second band starts at 92.19 (8.5e-11). Each
band is one Newton step that would be just under 1e-10, not taken: the stop is |step| < 1e-10 tested before the update. No constant
start (0 to 0.2 in steps of 5e-4, analytic or difference slopes, stops
1e-9 to 1e-12, test before or after) reproduces the 100 answers, so the
start depends on the price. Evidence: oz1.json.
Not landed: the start and the slope (analytic or difference, and its step)
are not identified, so no iteration reproduces Excel's bits. Next question:
bonds whose price is quadratic or linear in yield (one coupon period, zero
coupon) so the slope is exact and the stop rule separates from the start.
Evidence: evidence/ld1/oy1.json, oy2.json, back2-0.json, scripts/.

R49 / Fable-1 exponent scan (GPT-6.1 Sol (Codex), dna-firehorse)

6,006 valid zero-coupon ODDFYIELD questions cover six n=2/4/8 designs at
each exponent -500..500, scaling price and redemption together. Settlement
is a coupon date, issue is the previous coupon date, firstcoupon the next.
The long first coupon has zero payment, so both functions have the same
remaining zero-coupon price. All 6,006 answers are numeric and bit-identical
to YIELD, including the price80 negative root, both plateaus and every
crossover. This supports common solver behaviour for this sampled price
function; it does not prove a globally shared implementation. The earlier
equal-issue/settlement trial returned 6,006 NUM errors and is retained as
invalid-date controls, not numeric validation.

The proposed exactly regular first-period identity has a different obstacle.
In 303 scaled nonzero-coupon controls at frequencies1/2/4, every ODDFYIELD
answer is NUM. The 100 equal error/error pairs with YIELD are not numeric
evidence. A 19-case follow-up pins a narrow calendar discontinuity: for all
six frequency/rate combinations tested (rate0 or .05, basis0), issue exactly
one coupon period before firstcoupon gives NUM in BOTH ODDFPRICE and
ODDFYIELD; moving issue one day either way gives numeric answers. The ld1
corpus control repeats its exact original yield3fb3c65d22ec15b6.
This refutes the assumed availability of that regular-period comparison,
not shared iteration by itself. Other dates and bases remain untested.

The analytic backward experiment supplies no supported start or slope for
the shared zero-coupon behaviour. The ld1 untaken-next-step observation
remains a constraint, not a complete solver. No iteration change lands.
Evidence: evidence/round49, odd-scan-v2, regular-first-control, odd-calendar.
