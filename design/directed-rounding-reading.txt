TRUNC, ROUNDDOWN, ROUNDUP: the fifteen-digit reading at large scales
(ld1, Claude Opus 5.5, dna-koderbot, 2026-11-09)

Sources: the function bank (CalcFunc-Evidence, Excel 16.0.20430/20527).
Implementer; no tier 3/4 source read.

Where: ExactDecimal::fifteen_x87 scales |x| by 10^n, n = 14 - e, to read 15
digits. For |n| > 27 calc-func uses one 64-bit constant, the nearest to
10^|n|, and one rounding. The bank's larger differences (200 each in TRUNC,
ROUNDDOWN, ROUNDUP; worst 86 ulp) are all in that range, at tiny and huge x
with digit counts near 300.

Probe (research build, LD1_READ switch, full bank per function):
  model                                          TRUNC   ROUND
  current: nearest constant, one rounding        50,873  36,921
  nibble chunks, low first, x scaled step by
    step, each product rounded to 64 bits,
    negative n multiplies by 10^-chunk (S4)      51,217  36,925
  the same with high chunks first                50,941  36,925
  3-bit, 2-bit, 1-bit, 8-bit chunks              <= 50,933
  constant built first, then one product         50,913  36,897
  negative n divides by 10^chunk                 51,013  36,925
  products kept at 80/96/128 bits                51,017  36,937
  table entries built by repeated x87 products   50,690 / 50,337
S4 (the decomposition the publication step scaled_pc64 already uses) gains
396 rows in each directed function and loses 52; worst 86 -> 10 ulp. It is
not landed because of the 52 losses. Of 1,001 near-half full-digit rows, S4
reads 951; the misses split evenly: 24 where S4's 64-bit product is exactly
the half and Excel goes down, 26 where it is one unit below and Excel goes
up. Fitting each table entry's last bit (+-2 units) moves 2 rows only, so the
constants are the nearest ones; the open question is the rounding around
the final half decision, not the decomposition.
The research switch is evidence/ld1/decimal-reading-variants.patch.
