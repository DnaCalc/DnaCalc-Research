NORM.S.INV: design note (round 42, Claude Opus, dna-firehorse)

Sources: Excel answers retained in evidence/round39-41 (the R41 dense sweep:
128 windows of 8,192 consecutive doubles, 995,328 numeric answers), IEEE 754,
Wichura AS241 published tables. No tier 3/4 implementation was read.

1. What the mapping shows (before any program)

Regions, by the shape of the error against the true quantile (mpmath):
- p - 0.5 < -0.425 (p < 0.075): smooth sawtooth error, +1 ulp bias, the same
  answer for runs of adjacent p. It depends on p itself: pairs of p that share
  the stored q = p - 0.5 give different answers in every tail window.
- |q| <= 0.425: a function of the stored q only. No pair sharing a stored q
  differs (23,550 pairs in windows near 0.25, 0.2, 0.125, 0.1, 0.076). Odd: the
  windows just below and above 0.5 are exact negatives of each other.
- 0.925 < p <= 15/16: a third shape. Error sd 0.9 ulp, bias +0.8; Excel minus
  the AS241 tail is -1..+5 ulp (mean +2). One Newton step on Excel's NORM.S.DIST
  from AS241 does not reproduce it either.
- p > 15/16: the AS241 tail through 1 - p (R39), 1,283/1,283 retained answers.
The split at 0.925 is exact (window 72 changes at index 4096, p = 0.925), and
the split at 0.075 follows AS241's own test on the stored q.

Central noise. Within one window the error is Gaussian-like and uncorrelated
between neighbours, with sd growing steeply with |q|: 0.45 ulp near 0.5, 0.6 at
|q| = 0.2, 1.6 at 0.25, 3.9 at 0.30, 10 at 0.337, then 1.0 at 0.360, rising
again to 4.3 at 0.424 (both sides alike). Relative bias is +1.1e-16 out to
|q| = 0.25, then falls to -3.8e-16 at 0.337; after the break it restarts at
+1.5e-16 and falls to -2.6e-16 at 0.424. Excel's own NORM.S.DIST has 0.35 ulp
error there, so no root search on it explains the noise (tested: Newton/root
position, and two Newton steps on Excel's ERF give sd <= 1.1).
AS241's central rational expanded exactly into powers of q^2 and evaluated in
binary64 reproduces the noise sd within about 15% up to |q| = 0.337 (0.46,
0.49, 0.61, 1.8, 4.4, 5.0, 8.5 against Excel's 0.45, 0.48, 0.60, 1.55, 3.9,
5.8, 9.8), while its published r = 0.180625 - q^2 form stays near 0.7: the
noise is that of a monomial rational in q^2 with large alternating
coefficients. Its bias does not match, so the coefficients are not that
expansion rounded.

Near 0.5 (windows 64 and 65, 16,383 answers) one program is exact:
  t = 2q (exact);  y = RN53(RN64(t * K)),  K = RN(sqrt(pi)/2) = 0x3fec5bf891b4ef6b
  x = RN53(RN64(y * RN(sqrt 2)))
with zero mismatches; a single real constant (RN(q*C)) fails 26%, and the
AS241 near-half constants fail at least 35%. So the central branch is
NORM.S.INV(p) = sqrt(2) * erfinv(2p - 1), on the x87 at precision 64 with each
result stored, and the erfinv approximation leads with t*RN(sqrt(pi)/2).
The break between |q| = 0.337 and 0.360 is then |t| between 0.673 and 0.720:
the classic erfinv split at |t| = 0.7 (q = 0.35) lies inside it.

5. Round 43 (Claude Opus, dna-firehorse): central branch, still unidentified

Data: the 621,954 retained central and (0.925, 15/16] answers (R41 sweep,
R42 map) with erfinv to 34 digits (~/codex-runs/r43/central-truth.npz), and
four fresh 2,048-double windows at q = +-0.35355 and +-1/(2 sqrt 2) (asked
06:2x UTC, fresh/NORM.S.INV.split.replies.json).

Pieces, in t = 2p - 1 (erfinv's argument; the output is RN(RN64(y*RN(sqrt2)))
throughout, y the stored erfinv):
- A, |t| <= 1/sqrt2. The split is at t^2 = 1/2 to within four doubles on
  both sides (no error above 5 ulp past index 1020 of the upper window, none
  before 1026 of the lower); |t| = 0.7071 is refuted (both of its windows are
  all piece A). So the pieces are laid out in s = t^2: A on [0, 1/2].
- B, 1/sqrt2 < |t| <= 0.85 (y noise sd 1.4-3 ulp, bias -1.6..+2).
- C, 0.85 < t <= 0.875 (p in (0.925, 15/16]): y within one ulp of erfinv
  (RN 57%, +1 32%, -1 10%), bias +0.3 ulp. Accurate, not a cancelling form.
  It is not the root of Excel's ERF (the answer minimises |ERF(y) - t| among
  its seven neighbours in 353 of 772 sampled points).
Piece A in detail:
- Bias (window means, units 2^-53 of y): 0.89 t from 0 to t = 0.5 (that is
  K = RN(sqrt(pi)/2) and RN(sqrt 2) rounding alone), then a smooth deficit
  growing to 5.6 at t = 0.706 (about t^15..t^20). Taylor and Pade forms of
  erfinv(t)/t in s about 0 do not fit this curve (best chi^2 4,345 on 49
  window means, [6/13]); the deficit is more likely the coefficients'
  rounding seen through cancellation than truncation.
- Noise: consecutive answers sit on a lattice whose spacing grows smoothly
  with t (about 4.8 units of 2^-53 in x at t = 0.6, 9.6 at 0.673, 13.9 at
  0.706; no factor-2 jumps between 0.672 and 0.706), with spread about one
  spacing. A lattice wider than one ulp that moves smoothly with t means the
  last stored intermediate is several times larger than y and is then
  cancelled (relative spacing ulp(V)/y about 6..13 * 2^-53), as in the last
  Horner step of a rational with large alternating coefficients. A final
  product or quotient of stored doubles cannot do this (its relative spacing
  stays within one binade).
Candidates refuted (ranked by what a developer would have used):
- Giles 2010 single and double: one piece for all |t| <= 0.998 (w < 5 or
  6.25); refuted by the splits at 1/sqrt2 and 0.85.
- Blair, Edwards and Johnson 1976 (splits 0.75, 0.9375), Strecok 1968 (0.8),
  Boost erf_inv (0.5, 0.75), Cephes ndtri (p in [e^-2, 1 - e^-2], i.e.
  |t| <= 0.729, and q*sqrt(2 pi) near 0.5): refuted by the split positions;
  Boost and Cephes also by the near-0.5 program.
- MATLAB-style central rational to |t| <= 0.7 plus Newton: split refuted;
  Newton on ERF refuted by the noise (R42) and by the root test above.
- AS241's central rational, in q (16% of B, 10% of A) and rewritten for erfinv
  with P/(2 sqrt 2) (20% and 21%), each with binary64 or x87 stores; AS241's
  tail rewritten for erfinv (r = sqrt(ln 2 - ln(1 - t)), D*sqrt 2) on C: 13%;
  the AS241 tail at 1 - p on C: 10%.
Lattice map (fresh, 154 windows of 512 doubles at t = 0.400, 0.402, ...,
0.706, fresh/NORM.S.INV.lattice.replies.json): the spacing is not resolved
below t = 0.50 (noise under 2 ulp); from 0.50 to 0.706 it grows smoothly from
2.5 to 14.6 units of 2^-53 with no factor-2 step (one 17% step at t = 0.521,
within the estimate's noise). So the dominant stored intermediate stays in one
binade over that range and the growth comes from what multiplies its error
afterwards (a shrinking denominator, or further Horner steps in s). That is
a constraint for a fit, not yet a program.
Next questions: fit fixed-degree forms t*N(s)/D(s) (monomial Horner, x87
stores) jointly to the bias curve and the lattice spacing, ranking degrees by
the fewest coefficients that reproduce both; then prove on fresh windows.

6. Round 44 (GPT-6.1 Sol (Codex)): constrain the final cancellation first

Fresh 1,024-double windows at t=.52,.58,.64,.70,.706 (5,120 answers) test
y=stored(t*(C-stored(R(t)))), C a binary64 constant, then stored(sqrt(2)*y), without choosing C
or a series. If the cancelling quantities share a binade (or C is in the
next one), C-R lies on the exact grid ulp(R). For each proposed grid we
test every nearby multiple that could round to the observed output.
No window admits all its answers on a grid of 2^-51 or coarser (413, 482,
520, 706, 690 of 1,024 are admitted at 2^-51). Thus the large intermediate
cannot be just a final constant-minus-stored-series followed by t and
sqrt(2). This rules out that simple cancellation for any coefficients,
not only a fitted series. Earlier cancellation followed by t/D(t^2), or
several Horner stages that amplify its grid, remains possible. A rational
in 1-t^2 also remains possible. No degrees, pieces or constants were added.
Evidence: evidence/round44, normal-subtraction.*. This necessary lattice
test does not identify an inverse-erf approximation; the branch stays open.
